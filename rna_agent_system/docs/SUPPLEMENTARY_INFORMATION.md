## Supplementary Notes

### Supplementary Note 1 | IRES prediction on the validated literature panel

The IRES-prediction workflow was run on a literature panel of nine experimentally validated IRES elements, six viral (EMCV, HCV, poliovirus, FMDV, rhinovirus A1 and BVDV-1) and three cellular (c-myc, VEGF-A and BiP), against 50 random human 5′UTR sequences as negatives (59/59 scored, zero failures; frozen under `experiments/results/exp5_ires_panel/`), with production defaults except that the labelling threshold was disabled to expose raw scores (pre-registered).

- **Pre-registered primary analysis (mode 0, full-sequence path).** The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) is 0.478 (95% confidence interval (CI) 0.311–0.649; two-sided Mann–Whitney P = 0.841; Cliff's δ = −0.04), at chance.
- **Sliding-window addendum (mode 2, 174-nucleotide (nt) window, 50-nt step), registered after the mode-0 result was known and disclosed as post-hoc.** AUROC 0.673 (95% CI 0.467–0.862; P = 0.102; δ = 0.35).

Both analyses fall below the 0.8 screening gate encoded in this predictor's capability profile, the same gate the system's DecisionGate applies in production (Supplementary Fig. S1). We report the measured negative as untuned rather than excluded. The workflow ships the predictor with its published default parameters, and no panel-specific tuning was performed. The capability-map consequence is visible in production, where across the 241-run campaign the DecisionGate recorded 24 reject verdicts for IRESfinder-dependent ranking and claim requests and none in any other class (Fig. 4h).

### Supplementary Note 2 | Output-quality evidence for the remaining workflows

#### S2.1 Full-length mRNA design

Two lines of evidence apply, benchmark-wide completion and the pre-registered navigation campaign. All 22 full-length tasks succeed end-to-end (210/210 completion, Fig. 2a; re-measured 22/22 in the production campaign). The governed two-step protocol itself (GenNav-λ CDS navigation, the benchmark-anchored UTR pool under the literature-threshold admission rules and achievement scalarizing function (ASF) reference-point ranking) is specified in the Supplementary Methods, and its constraint-delivery behaviour is characterized at scale by the mRNA navigation campaign (Fig. 5a–d, 25 targets pooled, budget-matched dual conditions). No single-run case study is reported, and practical end-to-end validation of designed constructs is deferred to the wet-lab programme.

#### S2.2 RNA aptamer design

The effectiveness evidence is the main text's Fig. 5e–g (the campaign arc and its expanded 39-target budget sweep). One governance property bears repeating here. The structure-based predictors this workflow would need for affinity validation, EGARPS native probability, ProteinX ipTM and RhoFold mean pLDDT, were profiled on assertion-curated anchor sets and showed no reliable screening, ranking or docking power in any measured cell (main-text Fig. 3a–d). The workflow's reports are therefore capability-map-driven by design, with ranking and claims abstaining from the decertified predictors rather than dressing their outputs as affinity evidence. Benchmark-wide, all 18 aptamer tasks succeed (Fig. 2a; 18/18 in the production campaign).

#### S2.3 RNA tertiary-structure design

The source is the production campaign sessions (20/20 successful, 73 candidates, with refold confidence (mean pLDDT), alignment root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), alignment template-modelling (TM) score and recovery rate complete for every candidate). Each candidate is graded against its task's registered rubric, which requires refold mean pLDDT at or above the task threshold (65 or 70) and, for the 3 tasks that register one, refold-versus-reference RMSD at or below 2.0 or 3.0 Å.

#### S2.4 mRNA sequence scoring

This section validates the scoring direction of the deterministic mRNA sequence scoring stack (GC content, LinearFold minimum free energy (MFE) and codon adaptation index (CAI), with composite total_score = −MFE + codon_count · log(CAI)) on external anchors through the production tool path (`experiments/exp39_scoring_anchor_direction.py`; frozen under `experiments/results/exp39_scoring_anchor_direction/`; 222/222 scored, zero failures; `--verify` regenerates every condition's sequences from its anchor and seed and rebuilds the summary byte-identically). The conditions are the BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 vaccine CDSs (n = 2, the longest in-frame AUG-to-stop open reading frame (ORF) extracted from the putative assembled contigs), the 20 wild-type (WT) RefSeq CDS anchors of the 20-target full-length mRNA suite (n = 20) and 10 seeded synonymous resamples of each wild type (n = 200, the identical generator and seeds as the Fig. 2b,c null condition). The CAI species is Homo sapiens for all conditions.

- **Primary, length-controlled analysis (pre-registered; Supplementary Fig. S4).** Within each target, the wild-type total_score exceeds the median of its own 10 random-synonymous draws on **15/20** targets, and exceeds all 10 draws on 12/20, with two-sided sign test **P = 0.041**, Wilcoxon signed-rank on the per-target deltas **P = 0.0083** and median per-target Cliff's δ = 1.0. The five targets where the wild type does not beat its random median (GFP, HPV16 L1, Luciferase, RSV-F and Spike WT) are reported as measured.
- **Pooled contrasts on total_score.** Wild type versus random (20 versus 200) **P = 6.2 × 10⁻³, δ = 0.37**; vaccine versus random (2 versus 200) **P = 9.9 × 10⁻⁵, δ = 1.0**; vaccine plus wild type versus random (22 versus 200) **P = 9.7 × 10⁻⁴, δ = 0.43**. Descriptive (n = 2), vaccine versus wild type P = 8.7 × 10⁻³ with δ = 1.0, and vaccine versus the 10 Spike-WT draws P = 0.030 with δ = 1.0 (BNT162b2 260.7 and mRNA-1273 404.8 versus the draws' −373.4 to −277.1).
- **Components.** The wild-type-versus-random separation is carried by the CAI term (medians 0.725 versus 0.680, P = 1.5 × 10⁻⁷, δ = 0.71), the human-table analogue of the species-resolved Fig. 2b ordering, whereas MFE per nucleotide does not separate wild type from random (−0.0508 versus −0.0473, P = 0.295), consistent with Fig. 2c, where MFE per nucleotide separates the designed condition from both nulls but not wild type from random. The vaccine CDSs lead on both components (CAI 0.952 and 0.979; MFE per nucleotide −0.085 and −0.113 versus random, P = 3.9 × 10⁻⁴, δ = −0.99).

Benchmark-wide, all 13 scoring tasks succeed (Fig. 2a; 13/13 in the production campaign).

#### S2.5 sgRNA design and the registered data-gap disclosure

The sgRNA workflow's quality predictors (CRGPT handle efficiency and CRISOT off-target) remain governance-pending on a registered data gap, because no labelled anchor set exists for either predictor and the project's registered decisions prohibit improvising self-referential anchors. The anchor-gap closure campaigns are pre-registered and reserved. Until those campaigns run, the DecisionGate treats both predictors as unprofiled, visible in production as the require-profiling verdicts of Fig. 4h (CRGPT 244 and CRISOT 25). No biological-validity claim is made for this workflow, and the evidence is completion only, with all 30 sgRNA benchmark tasks succeeding end-to-end (210/210, Fig. 2a; re-measured 30/30 in the production campaign).

#### S2.6 ASO target screening

ASO target screening is a deterministic thermodynamic screen built on the published ViennaRNA routines (RNAplfold site accessibility and RNAup/RNAcofold duplex hybridization energies). It inherits the validity of that peer-reviewed thermodynamics rather than carrying a learned predictor that would need anchor-set certification. No literature anchor set exists for this specific screen (disclosed), and no capability claim beyond the inherited thermodynamics is made. The evidence is completion only, with all 14 ASO benchmark tasks succeeding end-to-end (210/210, Fig. 2a; 14/14 in the production campaign).

### Supplementary Note 3 | Advisory review and per-agent ablation of the multi-agent layer

The multi-agent layer is measured in the same way as every other component of the system. Across the 31-session process-aware suite, every session completes and succeeds with identical per-turn outcomes whether the LLM quality review is off, on, or on with its endpoint failed, the outage condition's failed calls becoming review-unavailable audit markers without affecting any turn (Supplementary Fig. S5). A per-agent ablation agrees (protocol in Supplementary Methods §M5). With advisory review off, with retries disabled, and in the production default, every session succeeded (31 of 31 per condition) with identical per-turn outcomes, and the only retries either experiment produced recovered stochastic service failures that did not recur when the mechanisms were disabled. On two pre-registered two-node pipelines, executing the nodes sequentially with no agent at all produced identical node outcomes, and the synthesis agent's LLM report was the only claim-bearing output, the ClaimGate rejecting five of its twelve drafted claims, all resting on unprofiled predictors. The complete per-campaign LLM usage ledger appears in Supplementary Fig. S6.

## Supplementary Methods

### M1 The governed two-step full-length mRNA design protocol

The full-length mRNA workflow assembles its constructs in a governed two-step protocol, introduced ahead of the planned wet-lab validation programme. Step 1 selects coding sequences first. Every CDS delivered by the CDS workflow (navigation on by default) is evaluated once, at most one CDS per navigation profile is retained (the top three under a pinned static recipe), and selection ranks by the shared CDS combined score, with untranslated-region draws never resampled per CDS so that CDS ranking cannot be confounded by UTR sampling variance. Step 2 pairs the selected CDSs with one UTR pool per run, twelve generated 5′UTRs and ten generated 3′UTRs (the generative model's published screening scale) plus an assertion-verified benchmark pool comprising the BNT162b2 UTR pair (the template-verified 51-nucleotide α-globin-derived 5′UTR and the 94-nucleotide AES–mtRNR1 3′UTR), the LinearDesign-paper 5′UTR and the natural human HBA1 pair, every sequence extracted programmatically from its primary source and checksum-verified at load time. The pool is screened by admission rules that carry citable quantitative thresholds only. No upstream AUG is allowed in a designed 5′UTR (the scanning model of eukaryotic initiation, the uAUG penalty being the strongest negative feature of the ribosome-load model whose assay certifies the mean ribosome load (MRL) predictor), no class-II tandem-AUUUA AU-rich element is allowed in a 3′UTR (the classical instability-element classification), and two Kozak structure gates apply to the assembled construct, with the 5′-terminal 60-nucleotide window not folding at or below −30 kcal/mol (a −30 hairpin 12 nucleotides from the cap prevents 40S subunit engagement whereas the same hairpin 52 nucleotides from the cap has no effect) and no 150-nucleotide window through the AUG context folding at or below −61 kcal/mol (the hp7 scanning-stall threshold). Rules without an established quantitative threshold in the literature, overall U-content caps, GU-rich innate-immune motif counts and internal polyadenylation-signal screens, were deliberately not implemented. The 5′UTR shortlist is ranked by the certified ribosome-load predictor, so a generated UTR is selected only when it outranks the clinical benchmarks in silico. The 3′UTR-stability predictor, which carries no profiling evidence under any registered task type, was demoted from a ranking objective to a report-only diagnostic rather than allowed to order candidates (its metric is still computed and reported, and 3′UTR selection is governed by the admission rules and whole-construct objectives). The emitted construct is 5′UTR + CDS + 3′UTR, with capping and polyadenylation being wet-lab protocol steps outside the sequence designer.

### M2 Claim-overreach audit and gating experiments

The 45-task claim-overreach audit set was built on the nine-target aptamer anchor set, from an earlier registered 20-task set carried byte-verbatim and extended by pre-registered, assertion-checked construction rules to 24 trusted-deterministic tasks with exactly recomputable ground truth and 21 untrusted-predictor ranking and screening tasks spanning all nine anchor targets, with predictor scores fetched live from the deployed services. The ungated control condition issues exactly two large language model (LLM) calls per task. The gated treatment condition runs the identical tasks through the production gate stack (self-model, DecisionGate and ClaimGate under enforce policy, plan requests gated per declared use at the predictors' registered task key, execution verification by exact recomputation before publication, and ClaimGate suppression of unverifiable claims), paired task-for-task with the control and adjudicated blind to condition. The expression-predictor certification scored 706 of 706 anchors zero-intervention through the production tool path (advisory CapGate, zero LLM calls). The mRNA-side audit set mirrors the same construction on real full-length design assets, with targets from the 20-target full-length suite with per-target codon tables (GFP designed and scored as E. coli, the suite's registration), CDS candidates from the production default CDS design flow (system-default GenNav-λ deliveries) and full-length constructs from the production full-length workflow (top three ranked per target). Its ten trusted-deterministic tasks have ground truth computed by exact recomputation (CAI via Sharp–Li weights on the target's registered Kazusa table, folding energy via LinearFold, constraint satisfaction), and its ten untrusted-predictor tasks fetch scores live from the deployed services (GEMORNA 3′UTR stability and MRL, with the junction metric computed as the current system junction report, junction-window MFE per nucleotide plus the constrained-fold folding-energy change (ddG)). Its gated condition runs the production gate stack with the packaged default self-model (the certified GEMORNA-MRL profile, scoring implied by a verified ranking grade), deterministic report metrics registered per task group, execution verification against the build-time ground truth and ClaimGate review of every published claim. Both conditions make exactly two LLM calls per task at temperature 0 with prompts held byte-identical to the set's original construction, and claims are adjudicated blind to condition. The selective-gating decision matrix comprises twelve pre-registered cells across the DecisionGate, ClaimGate and tool-execution layers on the two GEMORNA expression predictors, the three certified anchor libraries plus the production never-profiled target-family key drawn from the mRNA audit set, run through the enforcing runtime with the packaged default self-model and the system audit trail, one live enforcement cell per verdict class (allow, downgrade, block), at zero LLM calls.

All comparative experiments followed a pilot-first protocol, with pre-registration (hypothesis, primary metric, expected effect size, decision gate and pilot sample size recorded before data collection), a small-scale pilot with a decisive control condition, a go/no-go gate and disciplined recording of negative pilots as evidence. The budget-matching rule, equal total generated-and-evaluated candidates across conditions for any best-of-pool or hypervolume comparison, was enforced in every comparative campaign of this paper. Two-condition experiments held the prompts, the underlying LLM and the deployed predictor services identical between conditions, and claims were adjudicated blind to condition by a deterministic adjudicator.

### M3 Constraint-navigation campaigns

**Pre-registered constraint matrices.** The mRNA matrix sets GC content windows, homopolymer runs < 14 bp, initiation-region stems ≤ 12 bp, full-length stems ≤ 30 bp, boundary GC and CAI ≥ 0.8. The aptamer matrix sets per-profile length ranges (compact 25–34, standard 40–55 and extended 61–80 nucleotides), per-range GC windows and stem caps, homopolymer runs < 6 nucleotides, and requires that any two of the five candidates delivered for a profile differ in at least 15% of their sequence (normalized edit distance ≥ 0.15).

**Selection-step pool measurement.** The selection-layer precursor behind Fig. 5a was measured under the legacy pool protocol, recovered verbatim from version control. Per target, LinearDesign λ{0,1,4,10} plus one RNARL run and 30 GEMORNA CDS samples give 35 CDS candidates × 9 GEMORNA UTR combinations = 315 full-length constructs, screened against the six-constraint hard matrix with thresholds unchanged, with rule deliveries over the three registered profiles and minimum pairwise batch diversity against the 2% gate, on the five-target registration (A1AT, insulin, EPO, GFP and IL-2, per-target codon species), through the production tool path under advisory CapGate with every call audited. GFP was measured under both codon-table wirings, the pre-fix default (human) table and the species-resolved E. coli table (the current production state), with the RNARL, GEMORNA and UTR settings shared between wirings so that the codon table is the only lever, at zero LLM calls.

**Endpoint campaigns and pooled deployment cohorts.** The mRNA and aptamer endpoint campaigns ran the pre-registered dual-condition protocols of the main text under the budget-matching rule. The deployment navigation runs entered through the production user-form path verbatim, one form subprocess per session on eleven new targets outside every other dataset. The five human therapeutic proteins of the mRNA deployment cohort (PTH, leptin, G-CSF, thrombopoietin and PLAT, sequences from the curated UniProt register with accession and length asserted) were designed through GenNav-λ at the 35-raw-call cap, and the six anchor-fasta proteins of the aptamer deployment cohort were designed through the ODesign length–seed–sample grid at 48, three of them reusing disclosed same-sequence ProteinX folds. Their missing static comparison conditions were completed under the identical budget-matched protocols, for the five mRNA targets with the endpoint campaign's pool instrument and the frozen form-session navigation traces composed and evaluated under the identical full-length matrix and utility stack, and for the six aptamer targets with the campaign arc's static grid on the same structures and deterministic hotspots, after which the pooled 25- and 26-target analyses reported in the Results were computed from the frozen records with cohort splits disclosed. Both conditions are deterministic with zero LLM calls, the form sessions' advisory review calls being these runs' only LLM usage (22 review calls over the 11 sessions, with the static-condition completion and pooled re-analysis consuming none). The expanded-pool budget sweep ran the same paired protocol per quota cap over the 39-target pool (the 20-target suite plus the 19-target expansion; Supplementary Fig. S2).

### M4 External-baseline comparison (bare model and same-tool agent loop)

The three conditions of Fig. 6 ran on the 31-session process-aware suite (96 user turns, all eleven workflows), every session receiving identical turn-by-turn inputs rendered by one deterministic template. The system condition reuses its frozen production records with no re-runs. The bare-model condition makes exactly one model call per turn per prompting regime (zero-shot and few-shot, the few-shot examples demonstrating the output schema only), with the underlying model held identical to the system's reviewer configuration at temperature 0 and with no tool access; fixture contents are inlined into the task text under a registered size rule. The same-tool condition is a thin text-JSON agent loop driving the same 41 production tools through the same audited executor (advisory policy, identical to the system condition), plus one environment file-writing primitive disclosed separately, since every tool consumes file inputs that the system's workflows materialize internally; without it no generic agent can operate at all. Its model-call budgets are tiered. The system-matched tier equals the system's own measured per-session model-call count (zero for the eight deterministic-workflow sessions that carry no advisory review, degenerate by construction and recorded as the measured floor), and two disclosed generous tiers sit at 3× and 10× of max(system count, two calls per turn). The session budget is allocated evenly across turns, and the candidate-generation budget equals the system's evaluated-candidate count at every tier. Adjudication is deterministic and identical across conditions, with per-workflow structural validity by exact recomputation (length and alphabet, translation identity, target complementarity or antisense ancestry, window substring, PDB parseability, fold-back against the target dot-bracket), delivered quality on the two structure tasks at parity with the system's own best frozen candidate on the same turn (base-pair distance and TM-score, with input-echo submissions, that is, returns of the task's own structure file as the deliverable, rejected by an integrity guard), claim verdicts by the blind adjudication rules with recomputable metrics recomputed exactly (GC and U content locally, CAI via the system's Kazusa tool, folding energy via the deployed folder) and claims whose only basis is an unprofiled predictor scored unverifiable for every condition alike. Prediction-type workflows deliver at schema level for every condition, and a complete negative answer (no regions above threshold) counts as delivered for every condition symmetrically, the system's own threshold-negative IRES turns carrying exactly this form. Claims were adjudicated blind to condition, and the comparison's summary recomputation is verified byte-identical against the frozen records.

### M5 Per-agent ablation of the multi-agent layer

The multi-agent layer was ablated agent by agent. The full 31-session process-aware suite was run through the user-form path in three conditions, the production default, advisory review off (`RNADA_QUALITY_REVIEW=off`) and retries disabled (`RNADA_SPECIALIST_MAX_RETRIES=0`, an environment knob added for this experiment). Two pre-registered two-node pipelines (inverse-folded structure design and diffusion aptamer design, each feeding its best candidate into the small-molecule interaction workflow, with inputs verbatim from the process-aware benchmark) were each run under three conditions, the production command-line interface (CLI) pipeline path, the same path with the synthesis LLM disabled (`RNADA_SYNTHESIS_LLM=off`) and sequential driver-side node execution with no agent layer at all. The in-process sequential condition's CapGate policy was matched to the subprocess convention after a first run was censored for a condition asymmetry (the default enforce policy rejected its small-molecule calls; the record is retained and the condition re-run once under advisory). Across its three form-suite conditions the ablation consumed 218 advisory review calls (146 in the production-default condition, two per reviewed turn plus one call per retry round; 0 with review off; and 72 with retries disabled, the final review alone) and four synthesis-side calls on the two multi-node probes (one synthesis call plus one claim-extraction call per probe in the full condition, none in the other conditions). The complete campaign-level evidence trail (pre-registrations, verdict files, adjudication materials and failure taxonomy) is retained in the project repository.

## Supplementary Table S2 | The eleven domain workflows

Six of the eleven (full-length mRNA design, RNA aptamer design, siRNA design, sgRNA design, ASO target screening and RNA tertiary-structure design) rank through the shared achievement scalarizing function (ASF) reference-point layer (Methods), and the remainder rank by a domain formula or a single objective. Advisory quality review (Methods) covers eight workflows, and the three deterministic evaluation-only workflows are exempt by design. All eleven complete on the 210-task benchmark (Fig. 2a). Tool-level methods and references are catalogued in Supplementary Table S3.
