## Abstract

Computational design of RNA molecules with desirable properties requires chaining many specialized computational tools, work that today demands experienced computational biologists. Language-model agents promise to automate this process, but current scientific agents trust tool predictions without validating them, claim more than their evidence supports, and report gains confounded by unequal sampling budgets. Here we present RNADesignAgent, a multi-agent system for the design of diverse RNA molecules in which capability-layer evidence gating (CapGate) profiles every predictor against audited anchor sets before use and gates unsupported actions and claims, and generation-layer constraint navigation (GenNav) steers generators under matched budgets to deliver candidates satisfying multi-constraint design requirements. Capability gating eliminated false-positive claims (0% versus 54.5% ungated) while preserving true ones, and constraint navigation significantly improved constraint-satisfying delivery at equal budget across 25 mRNA targets (P = 0.009). RNADesignAgent resolves these failure modes, produces high-quality RNA molecules across eleven workflows, and offers design principles for future agentic systems.

## Introduction

RNA has become a therapeutic modality of central importance: messenger RNAs, small interfering RNAs, guide RNAs, antisense oligonucleotides and aptamers hold approved indications, or are in late-stage clinical trials, across metabolic, cardiovascular and infectious diseases. Designing an RNA molecule with desirable properties is the first and critical step in developing these therapies. Computational design can substantially reduce cost and shorten the design cycle, but because RNA design is a multi-objective optimization problem, it relies on experienced computational biologists chaining multiple tools to ensure the quality of the designed molecules. As the number of computational tools grows and their update frequency accelerates, even experienced computational biologists struggle to master the latest RNA design tools in time. This mismatch between tool abundance and expert bandwidth has become a bottleneck in the computational design of RNA molecules.

Large-language-model (LLM) agents are a natural candidate for automating this process, and recent systems have demonstrated agentic tool use across the sciences — in chemistry (ChemCrow, Coscientist), protein design (ProtAgents, the Virtual Lab), gene-editing experiment planning (CRISPR-GPT) and biomedical data analysis (BioMni). None of these systems covers RNA design. Moreover, deploying such agents in real applications raises a common problem: an agent inherits every assumption of every tool it calls and narrates its own results in natural language. Our experiments show that this structure produces three recurrent failure modes. First, uncalibrated tool trust: agents adopt a tool's predictive scores without validating whether those predictions have demonstrated power on the task at hand. Second, claim overreach: because agents write their own reports, their statements can exceed what the underlying tools support and are difficult to audit after the fact. Third, confounded improvement: agents often generate more candidates, so an apparent optimization gain may reflect nothing more than a larger sampling budget. No existing scientific agent profiles its deployed predictors before relying on them, checks its claims against evidence, or matches generation budgets when comparing strategies.

Here we present RNADesignAgent, a tool-oriented, calibrated multi-agent system for RNA design (Fig. 1). The platform wraps 41 scientific tools behind 21 local microservices and composes them into eleven deterministic domain workflows for the design of diverse RNA types. Two mechanisms counter the three failure modes above. Capability-layer evidence gating (CapGate) evaluates every (predictor, task type, target family) combination against assertion-curated anchor sets before use, producing a capability map that feeds two deterministic gates: a DecisionGate that forbids actions lacking an evidentiary basis, and a ClaimGate that weakens or suppresses natural-language claims exceeding the evidence. Generation-layer constraint navigation (GenNav) delivers candidate batches that satisfy pre-registered multi-constraint design profiles by navigating the generator's own control space under a matched budget.

Across RNADesign-210, a 210-task benchmark spanning all eleven workflows, the platform completed every task with biologically valid outputs — designed coding sequences, for example, exceeded wild-type codon adaptation (median CAI 0.896 versus 0.729; P = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶). Capability profiling proved two-sided: it decertified all three deployed structure predictors on the RNA–protein aptamer family while certifying an expression predictor on measured ribosome-load anchors, and the resulting gates eliminated false-positive claims on both flagship task families (0% and 7.7% versus 54.5% and 33.3% ungated) at a measured, decomposable true-positive cost. Constraint navigation, run deterministically at equal budget, won the registered mRNA paired discordance test against the rule-based executor (20:6, McNemar P = 0.009, pooled over 25 targets) and, transferred unchanged to a diffusion-based aptamer generator, mapped a two-sided operating regime with a significant win at starvation-scale budgets (10:2, P = 0.039).

## Results

### Overview of the RNADesignAgent system

RNADesignAgent is organized as seven layers (Fig. 1a) and operates as a general-purpose agent for the design of diverse RNA types: the user states a design goal in natural language and receives designed candidate RNA molecules together with their evaluation reports and a traceable record of the design process. At the base (L0), 41 scientific tools are exposed through 21 local microservices, so that every tool is invoked through a uniform, versioned interface. Tool specifications (L0), capability wrappers that validate tool outputs and write per-call audit records (L1), and eleven domain workflows built on a fixed generate → evaluate → score → rank skeleton (L2) are all deterministic, each workflow corresponding to the design or evaluation of one RNA type. A service manager (L3) maps each workflow's declared requirements to microservices, health-checks them and auto-starts what is missing; a multi-agent execution layer (L4) orchestrates, reviews and synthesizes workflow runs (below); and session memory and workspace management (L5) together with the CLI/FastAPI entry interface (L6) complete the platform (Fig. 1a). Because every design step is executed by deterministic tools and recorded to the audit trail, the complete provenance of every delivered RNA molecule — which tools were called, with what parameters and under which gate decisions — can be traced and replayed by the user. Two design commitments ride on this architecture (Fig. 1a, bottom): the language model never writes a sequence, so every molecule traces to a deterministic generator call; and every comparative claim follows a pre-registered, budget-matched protocol, so reported effects are attributable to mechanisms rather than to sampling effort.

The multi-agent execution layer (L4; Fig. 1b) comprises four cooperating agents. A PipelineAgent schedules multi-node pipelines deterministically. Per-workflow SpecialistAgents own strategy and retry; workflows with heterogeneous multi-objective candidate pools (six of eleven: full-length mRNA, aptamer, siRNA, sgRNA, ASO screening and tertiary structure) rank candidates through a shared achievement scalarizing function reference-point layer (Methods). A QualityAgent reviews outputs against per-workflow quality benchmarks, and a SynthesisAgent drafts multi-node reports whose headline claims pass through the ClaimGate before release.

Two mechanisms supply the platform's calibration. Capability-layer evidence gating (CapGate; Fig. 1c) profiles each deployed predictor on audited anchor sets before use and enforces the resulting capability map at two deterministic points: a DecisionGate at the single tool-execution choke point through which every Layer-1 capability call passes, which blocks predictor uses whose evidentiary basis is absent, and a ClaimGate on the SynthesisAgent's outgoing reports at Layer 4, which downgrades or suppresses claims exceeding the evidence. Generation-layer constraint navigation (GenNav; Fig. 1d) is implemented as a deterministic mode inside the Layer-2 workflows themselves — a λ-curve navigator in the mRNA CDS design workflow, inherited by full-length mRNA design, and a length–seed–sample navigator in the aptamer workflow — delivering candidate batches that satisfy pre-registered multi-constraint profiles under a budget matched to the static executor. Both mechanisms are production defaults of the platform rather than campaign-specific wrappers, and both are measured in turn below.

### RNADesignAgent generates biologically valid RNA molecules across eleven workflows

We first verified, using RNADesign-210 — our 210-task benchmark — that the platform's outputs are biologically meaningful across all eleven workflows (the eleven workflows are catalogued in Supplementary Table S2 and the 41 tools they call in Supplementary Table S3). Every task completed successfully (210/210; Fig. 2a). Four observations anchor biological validity (Fig. 2b–f). (1) On 20 wild-type protein targets, the coding sequences (CDSs) delivered by the platform's default design flow reached a median codon adaptation index (CAI) of 0.896, exceeding wild-type sequences (0.729; Mann–Whitney P = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶, Cliff's δ = 0.91), which themselves exceeded random synonymous sequences (0.675, n = 200; P = 6.8 × 10⁻⁷, δ = 0.67): the platform reproduces, and improves on, the direction of natural selection on codon usage. The same delivered CDSs were far more structured than either control (folding energy per nucleotide −0.297 versus −0.051 and −0.047; P ≤ 6.8 × 10⁻⁸, δ = −1.0 against both nulls), whereas wild-type and random sequences were indistinguishable on this metric (P = 0.30) — designed sequences therefore lead on codon adaptation and stability at once (Fig. 2b,c). (2) Inverse folding against target secondary structures recovered the exact target structure in 21 of 25 benchmarks (Fig. 2d); each of the four misses differed from its target by whole base pairs only — one gained pair in the partition-function-mode and 54-nucleotide tasks, two lost pairs in the combined-parameter task and three lost pairs in the 70 °C task (Hamming distances of 2, 2, 4 and 6 dot-bracket positions on targets of 6–16 base pairs) — and in each miss no candidate in the ranked set was exact, so the shortfall is generation-limited rather than ranking-limited. (3) On 22 siRNA design tasks, all but one delivered a best-ranked candidate with zero predicted off-targets (Fig. 2e). The sole exception — the identity-70 edge task — carried three predicted hits, and all three are perfect full-length alignments (zero mismatches over 21 nucleotides): the task's synthetic target is itself a verbatim fragment of the human lncRNA DDX11L2, a segmentally duplicated locus, and the delivered 21-mer occurs in exactly three of the 254,070 reference transcripts — the DDX11L2 source transcript and its two identical paralogous copies in DDX11L16 — so the scanner faithfully reports the guide's own locus and its paralogs rather than spurious partial matches. Scan evidence is trace-verified rather than assumed: two inputs request and execute a transcriptome scan against the human GENCODE v46 reference (254,070 transcripts) — one BLAST search returning clean, one bowtie2 search returning the three hits — while the remaining twenty zeros are scan-skipped (nineteen disable the scan by input specification; one — the SARS-CoV-2-target task — requests a scan but its species ships no registered reference). (4) The small-molecule binding predictor displayed the two-sided boundary its capability profile encodes (Fig. 2f): through the deployed service it reproduces its published held-out evaluations exactly (AUROC 0.974, 95% CI 0.968–0.980, n = 1,492 ROBIN pairs; 0.983, 0.961–0.997, n = 170 PDB pairs; the true ligand ranking second of 1,601 and seventh of 265 in the two published decoy case studies), whereas short-aptamer panels outside the training distribution show no demonstrated power (0.722, 0.278–1.0 against random chemistry; 0.563, 0.063–1.0 against near-cognate ligands; both intervals spanning chance). The corresponding evidence for the remaining seven workflows — full-length mRNA design, aptamer design, sgRNA design, ASO target screening, IRES prediction, RNA tertiary-structure design and mRNA sequence scoring — is reported in the Supplementary Information (Supplementary Notes 1 and 2), including the validated IRES literature panel, on which measured discrimination fell below this predictor's profiled screening gate and is reported as such.

End-to-end, every campaign ships a complete per-tool-call audit trail: the 241-run production campaign (all 210 benchmark tasks plus the 31 regression sessions, each a separate form-entry subprocess) deposited 24,897 audit events — 19,848 tool calls (79.7%; 40 distinct tools; 3 failures, each an isolated transient within an otherwise successful run), 4,862 gate decisions (19.5%; 1,800 reject, 489 require-profiling, 1,391 downgrade and 1,182 allow across 11 predictors; the non-zero allow class is the 3′UTR-stability predictor's annotation use after its demotion from ranking) and 187 ranking-objective assemblies. These aggregates are inspectable rather than opaque: every event class resolves call-by-call in a representative session selected from the same trail — a three-turn full-length mRNA form session whose idle gaps the audit log records (Fig. 2g; 1,853 calls, 882 gate decisions — 295 reject, 17 require-profiling, 298 downgrade, 272 allow — and 3 ranking assemblies over a 2,738-event audited span). Full design campaigns complete in minutes to about an hour per target: across 241 timed end-to-end runs through the user form entry — all 210 benchmark tasks plus the 31 regression sessions — the pooled median runtime was 2.5 min, workflow medians spanned 2.8 s (deterministic mRNA scoring) to 8.9 min (full-length mRNA design), 70.1% of runs finished within 5 min, 89.2% within 10 min and 239 of 241 within 1 h; the two exceptions are the 856-amino-acid constraint-navigation CDS design task at 70.7 min — the largest navigation target in the suite — and the constraint-navigation aptamer session at 72.3 min (Fig. 2h).

### Uncalibrated trust, claim overreach and confounded improvement in current scientific agents

Current scientific agents tend to adopt the scores of the predictors they wrap directly as optimization objectives, without asking whether a given score is calibrated for the design task it is asked to serve. Here we show that such uncalibrated scoring can mislead an agent's designs, and we give experimental evidence for three resulting failure modes, each quantified on the live platform as shipped (Fig. 3): uncalibrated trust corrupts the agent's actions, claim overreach corrupts the language in which it reports them, and confounded improvement corrupts its own evidence of self-improvement.

**Uncalibrated tool trust.** We profiled the three structure-based predictors deployed in the aptamer workflow — EGARPS+ native probability, ProteinX ipTM and RhoFold mean pLDDT — on assertion-curated anchor sets: 95 sequence entries spanning nine protein targets (67 binders, 62 of them with a measured Kd, and 28 negatives; FGF2 contributes ten table-printed binders spanning 2.2 orders of Kd, swine influenza H3N2 HA four, and IFN-γ a six-member same-family series; two further surveyed targets failed sequence printability at the curation gate and were rejected), together with 19 RNA–protein complexes with solved native structures across 14 independent targets for docking (SOMAmer-class and mirror-image chemistries rejected at curation with verified reasons). Each predictor–target combination was graded against pre-registered thresholds for three capabilities — screening (binder-versus-negative AUROC > 0.8), ranking (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6 against measured Kd) and docking (native pose top-1 in ≥ 2/3 of complexes) — and every measurement ran zero-intervention through the deployed RhoFold → ProteinX → EGARPS service chain as shipped (95/95 screening entries, 54/54 affinity-series entries and 397/399 docking poses scored; the two pose failures were reproducible service-side and are disclosed). No predictor showed reliable power in any capability. Screening (Fig. 3a): 2 of the 27 predictor–target combinations nominally exceed the gate — ProteinX on swine H3 HA (AUROC 0.83, 95% CI 0.33–1.0) and EGARPS on IFN-γ (0.83, 0.50–1.0) — both from small samples, and all 27 bootstrap intervals cover chance; FGF2, the widest-Kd-span target, stays sub-gate for all three predictors (0.37, 0.63 and 0.30). Ranking (Fig. 3b): 4 of 27 combinations nominally reach ρ ≥ 0.6 — all three predictors on the IFN-γ series (ρ = 0.71, 0.66 and 0.60, n = 6) and ProteinX on Angiopoietin-2 (ρ = 1.0, n = 3) — yet none survives scrutiny: the three IFN-γ intervals span zero and that series is a single in-silico-mutant family spanning under one order of magnitude of Kd (a within-family covariation regime registered at curation), while the Angiopoietin-2 estimate rests on three points whose scores flip between ~0 and ~1 across stochastic complex realizations. No predictor passes on more than one target.

Docking failed at scale (Fig. 3c): across the 19 complexes — the seeded rigid-body decoy protocol offline-calibrated, its contact computation reproducing the 1HVU native count of 152 exactly — the native pose ranked top-1 in 0 of 19, sitting at ranks 6–20 of 21 candidates and scoring below the median decoy in most complexes (the HIV-1 RT T1.1 native at 0.008 versus a decoy median of 0.34). An affinity-calibration panel returned the same verdict from the Kd side (Fig. 3d; 95/95 entries re-scored): the six-point thrombin Kd series reached ρ = 0.49, below the ranking gate, and pooled binder-versus-negative separation was at chance (AUROC 0.52, 95% CI 0.39–0.65; 62 binders versus 28 negatives). The one consistent pattern is per-entry inversion — the marketed drug pegaptanib scores ~1.3 × 10⁻⁶ while a scrambled TBA scores 0.98 — and all three score classes (binders, literature negatives and random sequences) span the full nine-order-of-magnitude score range with interleaved medians (0.22, 1.4 × 10⁻³ and 2.2 × 10⁻² respectively). The deployed predictor is therefore uncalibrated rather than reliably inverted: an agent optimizing against it would be climbing noise. Across screening, ranking, docking and affinity calibration the conclusion is the same: the deployed, uncalibrated predictors are not reliable optimization objectives, and an agent that optimizes against them may raise its scores without obtaining a genuinely better RNA molecule.

**Claim overreach.** Because agents narrate their results, the second failure mode enters through language (Fig. 3e). On a 45-task audit set of design-reporting tasks — the registered 20-task set carried byte-verbatim and extended by pre-registered construction rules across all nine anchor targets — an ungated agent (same prompts, same underlying LLM, exactly two calls per task, predictor scores fetched live from the deployed services) issued false-positive claims at a rate of 54.5% (30 of 55 claims, 95% CI 41.5–67.0%), and its plans or statements exceeded the scope the evidence sanctioned 59 times — 8–11 events per predictor and intended use (ranking or screening). The failure is class-selective: the 24 trusted-deterministic tasks produced 23 claims, every one verified true by exact recomputation, whereas on the 21 untrusted-predictor tasks whose scores come from the structure predictors profiled above, 30 of 32 claims were false (93.8%; ranking 16/17, screening 9/10) — the agent names the predictor's top scorer as the best binder, which the withheld Kd ground truth contradicts. Nor is the failure an artefact of task construction: the two benchmark strata — the legacy tasks carried byte-verbatim and the rule-extended tasks — fail at the same false-positive rate (15/27 = 55.6% versus 15/28 = 53.6%, the single borderline legacy statement excluded from both counts).

**Confounded improvement.** The third failure mode we found in our own earlier results (Fig. 3f). RNADesignAgent's bounded coordination loop — a deterministic, rule-based multi-round expansion that we removed from the platform once its gain was falsified — is here reconstructed verbatim at the experiment-driver level as the object of study and run against the production tool path across the full aptamer benchmark (20 targets × 3 seed sets × 4 conditions = 240 runs; 228 successful, the Ferritin zero-output boundary being the registered exclusion, leaving 56 paired target–seed units over 19 effective targets). The four conditions are the loop itself, the serial baseline, a budget-matched one-pass serial condition and a fixed-resampling control in which the loop's between-round diagnosis is replaced by a content-free regeneration instruction. On the EGARPS-component best-of-pool metric — precisely the predictor that profiling decertified — the loop's apparent advantage over the serial baseline is directionally positive and nominally significant (Cliff's δ = +0.14, paired Wilcoxon P = 2.6 × 10⁻³, 41 of 56 paired units; permutation-null P = 0.06), yet already below the pre-registered effect-size gate (δ ≥ 0.3), because each coordination round doubles the evaluated pool. The two pre-registered controls dissolve it: under budget matching the advantage falls to δ = +0.05 (P = 0.21), and replacing the diagnostic content with fixed resampling at identical mechanics and budget leaves exactly nothing (δ = −0.01, P = 0.73). A formal self-audit of our two strongest legacy claims under the four-step protocol — declare capability dependencies, re-check against profiles, control confounds, gated re-adjudication — retired both (claim survival 0 of 2 against a pre-registered 70% threshold). The three failure modes close in a circle: scores treated as objectives corrupt the agent's actions (Fig. 3a–d), the reporting language inherits the corruption (Fig. 3e), and the agent's own evaluation is confounded by unequal sampling budgets (Fig. 3f) — none of the three announces itself, and each became visible only under measurement. We treat budget matching as an iron rule for every comparative claim in this paper.

### Capability gating eliminates false claims while preserving true ones

The remedy for uncalibrated trust and claim overreach is CapGate, a deterministic two-layer gate fed by the capability self-model (Fig. 1c). The DecisionGate intercepts planned actions and forbids those whose evidentiary basis is absent from the self-model — for example, ranking candidates by a predictor with no measured ranking power; the ClaimGate intercepts outgoing natural-language claims and downgrades them along a scope ladder (absolute → same-target relative → trend-only → suppressed), enforcing "no claim without verification". Both gates are deterministic and every decision is appended to an audit trail, so the agent's epistemic behaviour is itself auditable. CapGate governed every comparative campaign reported in this paper, and it is the platform's production default rather than a campaign harness: the profiling evidence ships with the system as its default self-model; every call to a governed predictor passes through the platform's single tool-execution entry point, where it is checked against that self-model before the underlying service is invoked — so an unevidenced call is rejected before it executes rather than flagged afterwards; and predictors without profiling evidence are blocked by default until evidence exists.

The gates are only as good as the evidence beneath them, and that evidence is produced by capability profiling: before a predictor is trusted anywhere in the system, each predictor–task–target-family combination is measured against a curated ground-truth anchor set under pre-registered thresholds, and the resulting grades form the capability map the gates consult. Two properties of this evidence layer matter. First, it is two-sided: profiling withholds power that measurement does not support, but it equally *certifies* power where it exists — which is what makes the capability map a usable self-model rather than a list of prohibitions (Fig. 4a). The full-length mRNA workflow's expression predictor — the GEMORNA 5′UTR mean-ribosome-load (MRL) predictor — passed decisively on all three anchor families curated from a published massively parallel translation assay (706 anchors; Sample et al., GSE114002): the random 50-nt family lies inside the predictor's training distribution and serves as the registered positive control, and the two varying-length families carry the generalization evidence. Screening AUROC reached 0.990, 1.000 and 1.000 across the three families with bootstrap intervals clearing the 0.8 gate, and ranking ρ reached 0.926, 0.920 and 0.835 against measured MRL with intervals (0.75–0.95) entirely above the 0.6 gate — 706/706 anchors scored zero-intervention through the production tool path, the predictor deterministic end-to-end through the deployed chain. The same audit classified the platform's 3′UTR stability predictor as unprofiled — no measured-stability anchor set exists — and the DecisionGate accordingly blocks any ranking action that would rely on it. These grades translate directly into the gates' per-request verdicts, which we re-measured as a twelve-cell decision matrix on the two GEMORNA expression predictors through the production runtime (enforce policy, packaged self-model, every decision audited, zero LLM calls; Fig. 4b): ranking by the MRL predictor is allowed on all three families that profiling certified (confidence 1.0); the same request on a never-profiled family of the same predictor is downgraded rather than blocked — the self-model transferring the donor family's grade with confidence decayed to exactly 0.5, and the claim gate rewriting an absolute expression claim to within-target relative scope; and ranking by the 3′UTR stability predictor is blocked outright. The verdicts are the tool layer's actual behaviour, verified live: allowed and downgraded requests execute against the deployed service — the certified-family prediction reproducing the profiling cache exactly (MRL 6.28) — while blocked requests fail as gate errors before any service call; all twelve cells met their pre-registered expectations. On certified predictors the gates are thus selective rather than prohibitive.

The second property of the evidence layer is that profiling must target the *deployed* artifact rather than trust the publication (Fig. 4c). Together with the certified grades above, this is what the gates stand on: measurements of the system as shipped, not of what the papers report. The small-molecule predictor RNAsmol had initially failed to reproduce its own published performance on its own held-out test splits — a degradation with no error signal that only deployed-artifact profiling caught. Recovered byte-verbatim from version control and re-measured in an isolated sandbox on the same 1,662 official pairs, the pre-fix service scores AUROC 0.790 and 0.420 on the two splits (n = 1,492 and 170), agreeing with the registered incident record within 0.0094. Comparing the port's preprocessing against its pinned upstream commit revealed two deviations. The first is a case collapse: the model's case-sensitive RNA encoding (lowercase denoting modified bases) was upper-cased. The second is subtler: upstream fills the molecular-graph adjacency matrix by iterating only the first two entries of the directed edge list, with every index written shifted by one — so the adjacency the shipped checkpoints were trained on was almost entirely zero with two misplaced entries, not the molecular connectivity — and the port had "corrected" this quirk into the intended full symmetric adjacency, silently changing the input distribution the checkpoints were trained under. After the preprocessing repair, both shipped integration paths were verified live and zero-intervention on the same pairs: the default agent tool path (wrapper uppercase + T→U normalization, verified applied on every pair) reaches 0.975 and 0.986, and the verbatim service endpoint 0.974 and 0.983, the latter bit-identical to the registered evidence. The three measurements together also locate the damage: case collapse perturbs individual scores substantially (PDB split median |Δp| = 0.08, maximum 0.91) yet leaves in-distribution discrimination intact, so the predictor's collapse lived in the adjacency rewrite, not in case information. The deviation that looked obviously fatal was harmless; the deviation that looked like an improvement destroyed the predictor. Deployment infidelity is real, silent and layer-dependent — and which layer carries it is an empirical question that only measurement of the deployed artifact answers.

We evaluated the gates themselves in paired two-condition experiments — identical prompts, the same underlying LLM, predictor scores fetched live from the deployed services, and claims adjudicated blind to condition by a deterministic adjudicator — on two task families: aptamer design, on a 45-task audit set (24 trusted-deterministic tasks with exactly recomputable ground truth; 21 untrusted-predictor ranking and screening tasks spanning the nine-target anchor set), and full-length mRNA design, on a 20-task set built from real full-length design-reporting tasks. On the aptamer set, gating reduced the false-positive claim rate from 54.5% (30/55; 95% CI 41.5–67.0%) to exactly zero (0/20; CI 0–16.1%) — a hard guarantee of the gates rather than a statistical outcome, and it held on every task (Fig. 4d). The per-claim adjudication shows where the claims went (Fig. 4e): 31 claims reached the claim gate, of which 24 were published (20 adjudicated true, 4 borderline trend statements) and 7 were suppressed, with two further candidate sequences abstained by the execution-verification leg before publication; the gated condition carries no false claim on any task, and every claim that would have rested on an unprofiled structural predictor was blocked at the plan layer, downgraded to a borderline trend statement or suppressed. Decision-layer overreach events fell from 59 to zero (Fig. 4f): the decision gate blocked all 71 plan requests — every declared ranking, screening or docking use of the three decertified structural predictors, plus every deterministic-proxy and hallucinated-predictor request — so the gated agent never even fetched the evidence it could not support. The cost of the guarantee is equally measurable. True-positive retention was 80.0% (25 → 20), below our pre-registered 90% gate, and we report it as measured; the losses decompose deterministically — two true metric claims suppressed by the uniform basis rule (the model declared the deterministic metric itself as an evidentiary dependency, which gates as unprofiled: over-conservative, and a real cost of closing the free-proxy back door), two length-sloppy final sequences (59 nt rather than the intended 60) abstained by the execution-verification leg, whose claims would have been false had they been published (on those tasks the ungated condition had emitted correct-length sequences), and two lucky untrusted-predictor true positives that no calibrated agent can retain by design, because no structural predictor holds verified ranking or screening power — offset by one rescued true positive on a task where the ungated condition had made no true claim. A pilot of this experiment had initially failed because the model, when blocked, substituted an undeclared free proxy — a protocol lesson now encoded in the pre-registration template: gating must close every unverified claim path.

The result replicates on the mRNA-side set (Fig. 4g). We built the 20-task mRNA-side audit set from real full-length design-reporting tasks — ten with exactly recomputable ground truth (codon adaptation, folding energy, constraint satisfaction) and ten whose evidentiary basis the capability profile denies or degrades — and ran both conditions under the same two-condition, blind-adjudicated protocol through the current production assets (platform-default GenNav CDS deliveries, two-step full-length workflow constructs, the live predictor chain, and measured-MRL ground truth from the certified anchor set). Gating reduced the false-positive rate from 33.3% (6/18; 95% CI 16.3–56.3%) to 7.7% (1/13; CI 1.4–33.3%), a 76.9% relative reduction, with decision-layer overreach falling from 5 to 0 and true-positive retention at 12/12 = 100% — both pre-registered go gates (≥50% false-positive reduction, ≥90% true-positive retention) pass on this family. The retention contrast with the aptamer side is itself a governance effect: the two-step workflow no longer ranks on the unprofiled 3′UTR predictor (demoted to a report-only diagnostic), so the never-profiled-family expression claims that previously left the true-positive count through relative-scope rewrites no longer arise — the three cross-family claims in this task set adjudicate neutral in both conditions. The protection operates one layer earlier than on the aptamer side: five decision-gate blocks (3′UTR-stability and junction metrics declared as ranking objectives by the task prompts) left the model with no evidentiary path, and it published no claim at all on those tasks rather than fabricating one; one further 3′UTR claim was suppressed at the claim gate. The one surviving false positive traces to a predicted-versus-measured MRL rank flip on the weakest certified family (ρ = 0.835) — a ground-truth property of the certified predictor itself, faced symmetrically by both conditions. The same gate semantics operate at production scale (Fig. 4h): in the frozen audit slice of the 241-run production campaign, the decision gate issued 4,862 verdicts across eleven predictors, every one traceable — 1,800 rejects concentrated on the decertified predictors (RNASTOP stability 1,271, RhoFold pLDDT 302, ProteinX ipTM 101, EGARPS 66, IRESfinder 24, GerNA-Bind 22 and RNAsmol 14), 489 require-profiling verdicts dominated by the never-profiled CRGPT, RNASTOP, RhoFold, CRISOT and 3′UTR-stability predictors (244, 100, 96, 25 and 24), and 1,391 downgrades, all on the certified MRL predictor whose grade transfers to unprofiled families with decayed confidence — while the 1,182 allows are the campaign's only allow class: every one an annotation call to the demoted 3′UTR-stability predictor, permitted now that it reports but never ranks; no ranking request was allowed, consistent with no predictor holding a verified ranking grade for the campaign's production task keys.

The platform's own governance layers are, finally, held to the same standard — measured rather than asserted (Fig. 4i): across the full 31-session process-aware suite, every session completes and succeeds at the workflow level in all three conditions — LLM quality review off, on, or on with the review endpoint failed — with every session's per-turn outcome identical across conditions; the only retry in the matrix is a single on-condition turn that hard-failed after its first review call and recovered on re-run (0/1/0 retries by condition). The review-on condition completes exactly two advisory calls per reviewed turn (145 calls over 72 reviewed turns — the retried turn carried one extra call from its failed first attempt; the deterministic-exempt turns make none), and in the outage condition all 144 attempted calls fail into `review_unavailable` markers that are recorded as audit metadata without affecting any turn. The in-system LLM footprint stays minimal by construction (Fig. 4j): exactly two calls per task and condition in the three gating studies (90, 90 and 80 calls), zero in the selective-gating decision matrix, zero across all three re-run navigation campaigns (both conditions deterministic), a measured mean of 2.0 review calls per run across the production campaign (483 over 241 runs), 0, 145 and 0 completed calls across the advisory experiment's three conditions, and 22 further advisory review calls in the production-entry navigation runs over 11 sessions. The layer's agents are themselves held to the same standard in a per-agent ablation: across the same 31-session suite — production default, advisory review off, and retry disabled — every session succeeded in all three conditions (31/31 each) with every per-turn outcome identical across conditions; the review made exactly two calls per reviewed turn (146 across the suite, the two retry rounds included) and changed no outcome, the retry mechanism rescued the two hard-failure turns it met (stochastic service hiccups whose fresh runs in the ablated conditions did not recur), and the review-disabled and retry-disabled conditions made zero and 72 calls respectively (one final review per reviewed turn). On two pre-registered two-node pipelines the same ablation reaches the two remaining agents: executing the nodes sequentially with no agent at all produced identical node outcomes (the scheduler is behaviourally transparent on valid plans), and the synthesis agent's LLM report was the only claim-bearing artefact — twelve headline claims drafted across the two probes, of which the ClaimGate rejected five, each resting on never-profiled predictor dependencies (the two small-molecule predictors' screening scores), while the deterministic-report mode drafted none.

### Generation-layer constraint navigation efficiently generates RNA molecules that meet the design requirements

Where can an agent demonstrate value beyond a single tool's native reach? Single-metric quality (codon adaptation, folding energy) is essentially fixed by the generator's settings; within that range an agent adds nothing. The defensible agent task — arrived at through a failed experiment — is *multi-constraint batch delivery*: per target, a ranked batch of five candidates jointly satisfying a pre-registered constraint matrix (quantitative acceptance bands on codon adaptation, GC windows, homopolymer length and structure) under each of several design profiles in turn (for mRNA, expression-, stability- and balance-oriented profiles; for aptamers, three sequence-length classes). GenNav implements this at the generation layer (Fig. 1d). The generators expose discrete settings — for mRNA the LinearDesign balance parameter λ, which trades codon optimality against folding stability; for aptamers the length, random seed and sample count of the diffusion generator — and GenNav first spends a few trial designs to establish which of these settings can meet the constraints for the target at hand, before committing the campaign's quota of generation calls (the number of generator invocations each strategy may spend, held equal between comparison conditions). Delivery is profile-specific, so the alternatives a user receives are separable by construction; any two candidates in a delivered batch differ in at least a minimum fraction of positions (a sequence-diversity floor that prevents near-duplicate designs); and a target for which no setting proves feasible is reported as infeasible with its failure reason rather than silently dropped.

A first attempt had placed navigation in the selection layer: generate a large fixed collection of molecules once, then filter and rank it — 315 full-length constructs per target (35 coding sequences × 9 UTR combinations). This fails for reasons structural to the generation layer (Fig. 5a). On the five registered targets, the number of constructs passing all constraints ranged from 306 of 315 (EPO) and 297 (IL-2) through 144 (insulin) and 27 (A1AT) to zero (GFP), the sliding-window GC rule the dominant binding constraint — a factor of eleven between the best and worst non-zero targets, and a complete collapse on GFP. GFP's pool stays at zero until a species-matched *E. coli* codon table is supplied (0/315 → 18/315, the passing candidates confined to the two most stability-weighted λ settings): no selection-layer ranking can recover candidates the generator never produced. And the batches that do assemble saturate a single generator setting — insulin's five delivered candidates differ pairwise by as little as 1.01% of positions, below the 2% diversity floor. The shipped answer is therefore the deterministic GenNav-λ mode of the mRNA workflow, which navigates inside the generation layer: a probe sweeps a λ-curve across 35 preset values, with guaranteed repair and interstitial reinforcement, under a quota of generator calls matched to the static recipe's spend (35 calls: 4 LinearDesign λ values + 1 RNARL + 30 GEMORNA samples); every constraint dimension carries its registered acceptance band (CAI ≥ 0.80, CDS-level stem ≤ 30 bp, the sliding-GC window, homopolymer < 14); and a synonymous-diversity step — local codon substitutions that cost no generation calls — breaks the near-identical structure of same-λ designs so that delivered batches can meet the diversity floor. Each target is designed under all three profiles, giving one paired cell per target × profile per condition; the static condition (the rule condition) spends its identical 35 calls on the fixed recipe. Across 25 targets — the 20-target pre-registered suite plus five therapeutic proteins (PTH, leptin, G-CSF, thrombopoietin and PLAT) that entered through the production user-form entry, their static conditions completed under the identical budget-matched protocol — the paired discordance test favours navigation: 20 of the 26 cells delivered by exactly one condition go to navigation (exact McNemar P = 0.009, n = 75; Fig. 5b,c; the deployment cohort alone contributes 3:0), the joint delivery criteria clear on 46 of 75 cells against the static condition's 32, and 22 of 25 targets receive JCD-passing delivery, nine of them at 3/3 profiles — including HER2, on which the static pool structurally cannot deliver. Navigation spends well under its quota (mean 23.1 of 35 — fifteen targets stop early at 11–28 calls, the five deployment campaigns among them at a mean of 17.2, and ten run to the cap; zero violations, zero LLM calls). Two pre-registered disclosures accompany the win (Fig. 5d): the stricter gene-level criterion — one target delivering passing batches in all three profiles, gated at ≥ 0.8, a legacy-era calibration — is met on 12 of 25 targets (0.48, against the static pool's 1.00 as delivery context); and with the constraint-rate criterion met (0.96 against its ≥ 0.9 gate), the two conditions' composite quality scores are statistically indistinguishable (+29/=26/−20 signed cells across the 75 pairs, Wilcoxon P = 0.73) — navigation matches the static recipe's per-cell quality while delivering more cells. GFP remains the honest boundary in the other direction, contributing three of the six static-favouring discordant cells: its *E. coli*-table designs violate the full-length sliding-GC constraint under both composition rules.

To test generality beyond codon space, we ported GenNav — unchanged in mechanism — to the aptamer workflow, where post-profiling no structure predictor may rank candidates and the generator is a diffusion model whose per-setting quality is mostly sample-level noise (GC intraclass correlation 0.30; each probe sample adds only about 15 percentage points of setting-quality signal). A four-segment pre-registered campaign arc on the 20-target aptamer suite maps where that difference matters (Fig. 5e; both conditions budget-matched at 48 raw candidates per target, zero LLM calls, zero budget violations). Under a delivery-easy constraint matrix both conditions saturate — 56 of 60 cells each, one discordant cell apiece (P = 1) — while the navigator stops early, spending a mean 32.7 of 48 raw candidates for identical delivery (−32%); re-anchored to the production scarce matrix, discordance stays balanced (10:9, P = 1, n = 60); the decisive panel of the 12 most delivery-scarce targets moves to 10:3 in the navigator's favour (24 versus 17 of 36 cells delivered; end-to-end three-class delivery 4 targets versus 0 — the only completed targets on the panel; Fig. 5f,g) but not past the pre-registered significance gate (P = 0.092, a registered NO-GO, disclosed); and the delivery-abundant complement inverts the sign (1:8 favouring the static grid, P = 0.039). Completing the static grid on the six deployment targets — the same protein structures and hotspots as their form-entry navigation campaigns — pools the paired evidence to 26 targets × 3 profiles (Fig. 5g): navigation delivers 48 cells to the static grid's 42, with pooled discordance 18:12 (P = 0.36). The deployment cohort alone contributes 7:1 (8 versus 2 cells delivered, P = 0.070) and sits deep in the scarce regime: navigation ran to the 48-call cap on five of six targets (swine H3 HA the zero-delivery boundary under both conditions), and the deliveries it does make arrive in the closing rounds of its per-round records (IFN-γ stepping up at 29, 35 and 47 raw calls; PDGF-BB only at 36 and 48). Adaptive allocation therefore has a measurable two-sided operating signature, and the campaigns themselves supply the deployment rule (Fig. 5e): where the quota is scarce relative to the target's difficulty, navigation is decisively better — the pre-registered 39-target budget sweep's starvation cap delivers 10 cells to the static grid's 2 (b:c = 10:2, P = 0.039, the aptamer side's one statistically significant cell-level win, exactly where the starvation mechanism predicts; Supplementary Fig. S2), and it leads directionally at 20 (9:4) with parity at 24 (18 versus 15); where delivery saturates, navigation reaches the same delivery for about a third fewer generator calls; and where the quota is loose, the static grid's exhaustive per-setting depth delivers more cells (32 raw: 10:22, P = 0.050; 48 raw: 12:30, P = 0.008 — the regime in which generating everything is cheaper than deciding what to generate). The contrast with the mRNA side is mechanistic rather than accidental: LinearDesign's λ settings are deterministic, so a single probe reads them exactly and navigation wins outright (20:6, P = 0.009), whereas a diffusion generator's setting-quality mapping carries a per-sample noise floor that consumes any exploration budget at this scale.

### The platform outperforms bare-model and tool-using agent baselines across the eleven workflows

A complete platform has to be judged against what a language model alone, or a generic agent scaffold given the same tools, would deliver on the same tasks. We compared three conditions on the 31-session process-aware suite (96 user turns spanning all eleven workflows, identical turn-by-turn inputs): the platform as shipped, its records reused frozen with no re-runs; a bare-model condition (the same underlying model at temperature 0, prompted zero-shot and few-shot, with no tool access); and a generic ReAct-style loop given the same 41 tools through the same audited executor — plus one environment file-writing primitive, since every tool consumes file inputs that the platform's workflows materialize internally — under model-call budgets matched to the platform's own measured per-session usage, and at disclosed 3× and 10× tiers. Every turn was adjudicated by deterministic recomputation under one rulebook for every condition, the platform included — its frozen outputs were re-adjudicated through the identical checks rather than taken on trust, and pass on every turn (its four threshold-negative IRES answers count as delivered under the same symmetric negative-answer rule that credits a baseline's complete negative answer). A delivered turn requires structural validity per workflow (length and alphabet, translation identity, target complementarity, window ancestry, structure recovery) and, on the two structure tasks where quality is graded rather than binary, parity with the platform's own best frozen candidate on that same turn: a baseline's fold-back base-pair distance to the target secondary structure, or its alignment TM-score to the target tertiary structure, must match or beat what the platform's frozen best achieved on that turn — the platform's measured quality is the bar, fixed turn by turn in advance from the frozen records, with no threshold invented for the occasion. Natural-language claims were scored separately, by the blind adjudication rules, with claims whose only possible basis is an unprofiled predictor scored unverifiable for every condition alike.

The platform delivered 96 of 96 turns; the bare model 71 of 96 (zero-shot; 69 few-shot) and the tool-using loop 7 of 96 at the platform-matched budget, 44 at 3× and 71 at 10×. Because the platform delivered every turn, the paired discordance reduces to each baseline's own failures: the platform alone succeeded on 25, 27, 89, 52 and 25 turns (zero-shot, few-shot, matched, 3×, 10×), with no turn delivered by a baseline and missed by the platform — every comparison significant (exact McNemar, P < 10⁻⁶, n = 96). The failure modes are informative rather than uniform (Fig. 6a,b): the bare model's failures are capability failures — answers below the platform's structural quality on 14 turns, outright invalid on 11 (zero-shot; 13 and 14 few-shot) — whereas the loop's failures at the matched and 3× budgets are almost entirely budget exhaustion without an answer (86 and 42 turns): every step of the loop is itself a model call, and the matched tier's two calls per turn on the review-bearing sessions — the platform's own review cadence — buy a single tool round trip, one call to request a tool and one to act on its result, with nothing left for a second attempt. At the generous tier the loop's remaining failures are mostly quality failures (16 turns below parity, nine still exhausting budget). The two baselines tie in aggregate by winning different workflows (discordance exactly 12:12, P = 1.0): the loop wins exactly where computation is indispensable — tertiary-structure design (4 of 6 turns versus the bare model's 0), crRNA design (3 of 13 versus 0), coding-sequence and full-length design — while the bare model wins the small-composition tasks the loop over-spends on: antisense windows (9 of 9 versus 3 of 9, the loop exhausting its step budget on accessibility scans of a long transcript without submitting) and secondary-structure targets of 15–23 nucleotides that direct reasoning solves exactly while the loop's submissions land one to six base pairs off the platform's exact recovery.

Claim behaviour separated the conditions more sharply than delivery (Fig. 6c). The platform publishes no claims from single-workflow sessions by construction — its claim gate operates on pipeline synthesis reports — whereas both baselines assert freely: the bare model's recomputable claims were false-positive at 48.3% zero-shot (95% CI 43.5–53.1) but 11.4% few-shot, the worked examples anchoring the arithmetic it can do in its head; the tool-using loop's claims carried a 51.9% false-positive rate pooled across its budget tiers (51.1% at the generous tier, CI 44.7–57.5) — the ungated-control class measured among current scientific agents in section 2.3 (54.5%) generalizes from our own ungated pipeline to a generic scaffold — with 30–37% of its claims unverifiable outright.

The cost ledger completes the picture (Fig. 6d): the platform completed the entire suite on 146 model calls, all of them quality review; the loop needed 756 model and 659 tool calls at the generous tier — 5.2 times the platform's model budget — to reach the bare model's delivery rate. On the deterministic workflows (sequence scoring, small-molecule prediction, antisense screening) the platform uses no model calls at all, so the platform-matched budget for those eight sessions is exactly zero — and a loop whose every step is a model call cannot emit so much as its first action: all 24 turns there end in recorded budget exhaustion with no answer. We report this degenerate floor as measured rather than rescue it; the disclosed generous tiers, whose base is floored at two calls per turn, are where the loop first operates on these workflows — at 3× it delivers 9 of 9 small-molecule turns, 4 of 6 sequence-scoring and 1 of 9 antisense. Tool access alone, in other words, buys validity only where computation is indispensable, taxes it everywhere else, and reintroduces ungated claim behaviour; the platform's advantage — full validity at a fifth of the model cost with no false claims — comes from its deterministic workflows and governance rather than from model cleverness.

## Discussion

In this work we showed that three failure modes of agentic science — uncalibrated tool trust, claim overreach and confounded improvement — are not theoretical risks but measurable defaults of a deployed system, and then built RNADesignAgent as a tool-first countermeasure, with each mechanism aimed at a specific failure mode. Capability-layer evidence gating (CapGate) answers the first two: anchor-set profiling withdraws or certifies each predictor's power before use, the DecisionGate blocks actions whose evidentiary basis is absent, and the ClaimGate downgrades or suppresses claims that exceed the evidence. Generation-layer constraint navigation (GenNav), shipped as a deterministic mode and evaluated exclusively under budget-matched pre-registered protocols, answers the third — together with the platform-wide budget-matching rule that every comparative claim in this paper obeys. The measurements support each mapping. Profiling proved two-sided: it revoked every presumed predictor power we tested on the aptamer chain — no reliable screening cell (2/27 nominal passes, all confidence intervals covering chance), no reliable ranking cell (4/27, all vacuous) and docking failing in 0 of 19 complexes — while certifying the expression predictor on measured ribosome-load anchors and reproducing a small-molecule predictor's published performance once a silent deployment fault was repaired. Budget-matched self-audit retired our own two strongest legacy claims (survival 0/2), establishing confounded improvement as a default state of agentic evaluation rather than a rare accident. And the remedies work, at measured cost: capability gating eliminated false-positive claims on both flagship task families — 0 of 20 claims (95% CI 0–16.1%) versus 54.5% ungated on the aptamer set, and 7.7% (1/13) versus 33.3% (6/18) on the mRNA set, a 76.9% relative reduction — while preserving true ones (12/12 on the mRNA set; 80% on the aptamer set, below our pre-registered 90% gate and reported as measured). Generation-layer constraint navigation, run deterministically at equal budget, won the registered mRNA paired discordance test against the rule baseline pooled over 25 targets (20:6, McNemar P = 0.009, n = 75, zero LLM calls, zero budget violations); on the aptamer side the same mechanism mapped a two-sided operating regime rather than a blanket win — decisive at starvation-scale budgets (10:2, P = 0.039 on the 39-target expanded pool) and the only condition completing end-to-end delivery on the hardest panel (4 targets versus 0), with pooled delivery ahead over 26 targets (48 versus 42 cells) but pooled discordance at parity (18:12, P = 0.36) and the decisive panel's significance gate missed and disclosed (10:3, P = 0.092) — a boundary measurement that doubles as the deployment rule for adaptive allocation.

A central insight is that the locus of intelligence in a trustworthy agent is not the language model but the *evidence infrastructure* around it: the anchor sets, the capability grades, the gates, the audit trail. This is a principled response to how agentic systems actually fail: language is where overreach enters, so claims — not just actions — must be gated; sampling is where confounds enter, so budgets — not just outcomes — must be controlled; and deployment is where tools silently diverge from their papers, so the deployed artifact — not the publication — must be profiled. The multi-agent layer follows the same discipline: every retained agent's contribution is measured rather than assumed. In a per-agent ablation over the full 31-session regression suite — advisory review off, retry mechanism off, production default — every session completed in all three conditions with every per-turn outcome identical across conditions: the quality review is advisory by construction and was verified outcome-neutral at a fixed cost of exactly two calls per reviewed turn, and the retry mechanism fired on two turns and rescued both, the same turns succeeding without it in the ablated conditions. On pre-registered two-node pipelines the deterministic scheduler proved behaviourally transparent — sequential execution without any agent produced identical node outcomes — while the synthesis agent's report was the only claim-bearing artefact: of the twelve headline claims it drafted across the two probes, the ClaimGate rejected five for lacking an evidentiary basis, and with the synthesis LLM disabled no claims were drafted at all. And where measurement falsified a benefit outright — the coordination loop — the mechanism was removed rather than retained on plausibility. The same standard holds under external comparison (Fig. 6): on the identical 96-turn suite, the same underlying model used bare delivers 71 turns (69 few-shot), and a generic tool-using loop given the platform's own 41 tools delivers 7 at the platform-matched model-call budget and 71 at the disclosed generous tier — each condition paired against the platform's 96 of 96 with no reverse discordance (exact McNemar, P < 10⁻⁶) — while the loop's ungated claims rejoin the section-2.3 control class (51.9% false-positive against the ungated 54.5%) at 5.2 times the platform's model spend (756 versus 146 calls). The platform's advantage is therefore a property of its deterministic workflows and governance, not of the model itself. We also note what calibrated agency is not. It is not autonomous discovery: the system is a scientist-in-the-loop design instrument whose deliverables are candidates, profiles and audit trails, and we emphasize the distinction between calibrated tool orchestration and genuine scientific reasoning. Nor is it a claim that the underlying predictors are weak — only that their power is regime-specific, and that the regime must be established before, not after, optimization. Finally, because RNA design touches therapeutic development, dual-use considerations apply: the platform's deterministic gates, off-target scanning and per-call audit trails are guardrails in themselves, and we release the capability map so that others can inherit the boundaries rather than rediscover them.

RNADesignAgent currently has two principal limitations. First, constrained by how much design procedures differ across RNA types and by the availability of design tools and anchor datasets, the two mechanisms are exercised end-to-end on only two RNA types — full-length mRNA design and RNA aptamer design. GenNav is implemented in these two workflows, and the profiled predictors that back selection and ranking belong to these families; the platform's other workflows run under the same gates and audit trail, with their predictors held to profiled boundaries or require-profiling defaults rather than certified use. As design tools and anchor data for the remaining RNA types mature, both mechanisms can be extended to the corresponding design workflows. Second, the anchor sets used to audit and calibrate the predictors for selection and ranking in these two workflows, although assertion-checked and coverage-verified, remain limited in scale; richer anchor sets will further improve the calibration of the prediction models and, with it, the reliability of the performance they support. Overall, these represent areas for ongoing development rather than fundamental limitations.

More broadly, the artifacts released here — the capability map of widely used RNA predictors, the assertion-curated anchor sets, the budget-matched pre-registration templates, and the register of concluded directions — are reusable independently of our system. Any agentic pipeline, in any domain, can adopt the same discipline: profile before you optimize, gate before you claim, match budgets before you compare. We anticipate that calibrated agency will become more, not less, important as agents grow more capable, because each added capability enlarges the space of plausible-sounding but unverified actions.

## Methods

### System architecture

RNADesignAgent is implemented as seven layers. L0 (tools): 41 scientific tools, each defined by a Pydantic-typed specification (identity, version, endpoint, capabilities, limitations) and exposed through one of 21 local FastAPI microservices, one per external code family, each with its own environment and local model weights. L1 (capabilities): thin wrappers that validate tool outputs against their specifications and write audit records (unique per-parameter result keys, per-call timings) into the shared state. L2 (workflows): eleven domain workflows (mRNA CDS design, full-length mRNA design, mRNA sequence scoring, siRNA design, sgRNA design, ASO target screening, IRES prediction, RNA aptamer design, RNA secondary-structure design, RNA tertiary-structure design, RNA–small-molecule interaction), all subclasses of a base workflow enforcing a generate → evaluate → score → rank skeleton with progress events and partial-rerun support (the eleven workflows and the 41 tools are catalogued in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). L3 (service orchestration): a service manager that maps each workflow's declared capability requirements to microservices, with file-locked, health-check-gated auto-start. L4 (multi-agent execution): a deterministic PipelineAgent scheduling pipeline nodes; a SpecialistAgent per workflow owning retry decisions (hard failures only); the six multi-objective workflows ranking through the shared ASF reference-point layer described below; an advisory QualityAgent issuing per-workflow quality reports; and a SynthesisAgent for multi-node reports. L5 (session/workspace): per-session workspaces with context-propagated session IDs and cross-turn result history. L6 (entry): a form-driven CLI and a FastAPI interface through a unified runner; workflow selection is explicit, and user-supplied parameters are sanitized against workflow input models.

### Multi-agent execution and governance

The multi-agent layer comprises four agents with deliberately narrow roles; every retained agent's contribution is measured rather than assumed (Results). A PipelineAgent schedules the nodes of multi-node pipelines deterministically — input binding, critical-failure handling and final synthesis — and makes no LLM calls. A SpecialistAgent owns each workflow: it runs the workflow, obtains a quality review and decides whether to retry. Retries are confined to hard failures — a run that returned no candidates or whose tool calls errored — because these are objective, reproducible signals, whereas a reviewer's dissatisfaction is not: allowing an LLM's opinion to trigger regeneration would reintroduce exactly the unmeasured adaptivity that the platform's governance is built to exclude. A QualityAgent reviews each workflow's outputs against per-workflow quality benchmarks and issues an advisory report; it can neither trigger a retry nor flip a successful run to failed. The advisory design is deliberate: if the review call to the LLM fails, the report is marked review-unavailable and recorded as a zero-confidence pass with the error retained for audit, and an abort recommended by the LLM is recorded as advice only — in both paths the run's success is decided by the deterministic workflow, never by the language model. Three deterministic workflows (mRNA sequence scoring, ASO target screening and RNA–small-molecule interaction) are explicitly exempt from review. A SynthesisAgent drafts multi-node reports; every headline claim passes through the ClaimGate before release, so the only LLM-authored text that reaches the user is claim-gated. A bounded coordination loop formerly layered on top of the specialists was removed after a budget-matched control showed its gain was confounded (Results). The sole external LLM used by the platform is DeepSeek (deepseek-v4-pro at the official endpoint; temperature 0.2 for quality reviews, 0.0 when the LLM is itself under test); all structure and sequence predictors run as local microservices with local weights. Mechanized ranking removes the LLM from the navigation loop entirely — the navigation campaigns reported in this paper run deterministically with zero LLM calls.

### Candidate ranking: achievement scalarizing reference-point optimization

Every workflow whose candidate pool carries two or more heterogeneous objectives ranks through one shared implementation. Objectives are min–max normalized to [0, 1] within the candidate pool, and candidates are ordered by an augmented Chebyshev achievement scalarizing function (ASF) against a reference point *r*: ASF(s) = max_i {w_i (r_i − s_i)} + ρ Σ_i |w_i (r_i − s_i)| with ρ = 10⁻³, which guarantees Pareto-optimality of the minimizer; weights are uniform by default or renormalized from a workflow's existing weight parameters (falling back to uniform whenever a ranking dimension lacks a weight). The reference point is user-supplied in raw units or, by default, the utopian point (pool-best per dimension); a non-dominated Pareto frontier on raw objective values is always returned alongside the ordering (capped at ten members with per-dimension champions retained). The layer encodes three explicit missing-data policies. A dimension unmeasured across the whole pool — a predictor blocked by CapGate, or an optional evaluator disabled — drops out of the ranking for every candidate equally (the CapGate degrade semantics: components zero or vanish for all, never for some). A dimension missing for an individual candidate (a failed or skipped predictor call) is imputed at the measured pool-worst normalized value: omission from the Chebyshev maximum can only lower a candidate's ASF, so a ranking audit replaced the earlier omission semantics — which rewarded evaluation failures — with pool-worst imputation, which additionally leaves the normalization of the measured candidates undistorted. A user reference point covering only part of the measurable dimensions restricts the ranking to that subset and is flagged in the run summary rather than applied silently. Six of the eleven workflows use this layer — full-length mRNA (CDS quality, 5′UTR ribosome load, full-length folding energy; the 3′UTR-stability predictor was demoted to a report-only diagnostic — Supplementary Methods), aptamer (complex probability, predicted fold confidence, interface score), siRNA (duplex efficiency, specificity, and a nearest-neighbour thermodynamic end asymmetry that favours antisense-strand guide loading; the full pool is ranked before truncating to the requested count), sgRNA handle (CRGPT perplexity, CRISOT specificity), ASO target screening (site accessibility, duplex binding free energy) and tertiary structure (predicted fold confidence plus two reference-based closeness measures, RMSD and TM-score); the remaining five are single-objective, single-candidate or domain-formula tasks where scalarization is vacuous and rank directly. ASF values are pool-relative by construction (ranks are non-monotonic under pool composition, and the utopian reference can be attained, ASF = 0); cross-pool comparisons therefore never compare raw ASF distances — they use shared fixed reference points, per-objective components or Pareto hypervolume.

### Capability-layer evidence gating (CapGate)

CapGate is fed by a capability self-model (the SelfModel): a machine-readable register, packaged with the platform and built from the frozen verdict files of the capability audits, that maps each (predictor, task type, target family) tuple to one of four capability grades (screening / ranking / docking / none) with evidence pointers and confidence. The CapabilityProfiler computes grades from anchor-set evaluations against pre-registered thresholds (screening AUROC > 0.8; ranking Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6; docking native top-1 in ≥ 2/3 cases), with a deterministic driver backend. The DecisionGate intercepts planned actions and forbids those whose evidentiary basis is absent; the ClaimGate intercepts outgoing claims and downgrades them along a scope ladder (absolute → same-target relative → trend-only → suppressed), enforcing "no claim without verification". Both gates are deterministic; every decision is appended to an append-only JSONL audit trail. CapGate is a production default of the platform rather than a campaign harness: a process-wide runtime configured by the entry runner (environment switch `RNADA_CAPGATE` = off / advisory / enforce, default enforce) (i) records every tool call made by any workflow to the platform audit trail (name, arguments summary, success, timing, error, gate flag, and the session and workflow identity of the run that made the call, so every line of the append-only trail is attributable to its run), (ii) reviews the managed predictor tools — the aptamer structure predictors, both GEMORNA UTR predictors and the small-molecule scorer — against the SelfModel at the single tool-execution choke point, blocking calls whose grade is failed or unprofiled under enforce policy (workflows' existing fallbacks zero the affected component for all candidates equally, so runs complete and the predictor is neutralized rather than crashing), and (iii) passes the headline claims of multi-node synthesis reports through the ClaimGate, with claim extraction as the only LLM step. The profiling evidence travels with the system as a packaged default SelfModel built from the frozen verdict files of the capability audits, so production gating enforces the published profiling results out of the box. Family transfer in the SelfModel is conservative: a never-profiled (predictor, task) family inherits the donors' worst grade per capability. Anchor sets are assertion-curated: entries carry role labels (binder, negative, unrankable), internal consistency assertions and negative assertions are checked at build time, and coverage (≥ 4 binders and ≥ 3 negatives per target) is verified before use. mRNA-side anchors follow the same pattern with measured continuous labels (mean ribosome load) substituted for affinities, dichotomized at pre-registered pool extremes (top versus bottom 60 of the read-depth-filtered pool) for the screening grade, with a 120-anchor rank-stratified subset per family for the ranking grade.

### Generation-layer constraint navigation (GenNav)

GenNav comprises four components: (1) a feasibility-first adaptive probe over the generator's discrete settings that determines which settings are feasible for a target before the quota of generation calls is allocated, with an explicit infeasibility path when no setting is feasible; (2) profile-separable delivery — per-profile constraint bands for mRNA (expression/stability/balanced), per-profile length bands for aptamers; (3) a diversity mechanism enforcing a minimum pairwise edit distance among the candidates delivered for the same design profile — a synonymous-diversity expander for mRNA (local codon substitutions that spend no generation budget), greedy within-batch diversity floors for aptamers; and (4) explicit generator-context resolution (for mRNA, species-matched codon tables; for aptamers, length and seed settings). Constraint matrices are pre-registered per campaign (mRNA matrix: GC content windows, homopolymer runs < 14 bp, initiation-region stems ≤ 12 bp, full-length stems ≤ 30 bp, boundary GC, CAI ≥ 0.8; aptamer matrix: per-profile length bands compact 25–34 / standard 40–55 / extended 61–80 nt, per-band GC windows and stem caps, homopolymer runs < 6 nt, and a within-batch normalized edit distance ≥ 0.15 among the five delivered candidates per profile). The generation budget is identical across conditions in every campaign (the budget-matching rule): on mRNA, 35 raw generator calls per target per condition (the static recipe — the rule executor's fixed generator settings, 4 LinearDesign λ values + 1 RNARL run + 30 GEMORNA samples — versus the navigator's budget-matched LinearDesign cap, pooled mean spend 23.1 with early stop); on aptamers, 48 raw candidates per target per condition, the navigation condition spending at most four targeted reinforcement rounds of six candidates inside the same cap, with a deterministic guarantee rule that allocates one round to every repairable delivery band before any band receives a second. Reinforcement settings are selected from laplace-smoothed pass rates fused with the live pool at no extra budget. The decisive aptamer protocol is a production path: the aptamer workflow's opt-in navigation mode runs the densified length-grid probe, the cycling guaranteed-repair allocation and the per-profile delivery deterministically (zero LLM calls), folds every candidate through the audited LinearFold service for the constraint matrix, caps the generation budget at 48 raw candidates, stops early once every profile is deliverable, and writes the complete navigation trace (probe, rounds, per-profile deliveries, degrades) to the run record. The mRNA-side mode follows the same pattern, its parameters calibrated against a re-run of the pre-registered endpoint: a concurrent λ-curve probe over a densified 35-value λ grid, budget-matched reinforcement, per-profile matrix delivery whose every band dimension carries its registered counterpart value (CAI floor 0.80, CDS-level stems ≤ 30 bp, GC window at the registered sliding bounds, homopolymer < 14), the synonymous-diversity expander, and the complete navigation trace (probe, rounds, expansions, deliveries) in the run record — so a navigation campaign is reproducible from its audit trail exactly like every other platform action.

### Benchmarks and anchor sets

Four registered benchmark suites were used: a 210-task platform suite spanning the eleven workflows (RNADesign-210); a 20-target full-length mRNA suite of therapeutic proteins (two legacy oversized targets — the ZIKV polyprotein and factor VIII, both beyond the LinearDesign memory guard — replaced by OTC and PDGFB, with translation-verified RefSeq CDSs); a 20-target aptamer suite; and a 31-session process-aware multi-turn regression suite exercising all eleven workflows through the user form entry — also the task set of the external-baseline comparison (Fig. 6), whose three conditions run under the protocol registered in Supplementary Methods. Three further registered assets extend the two flagship suites. A 19-target expanded aptamer pool — 12 RNA–protein complexes from the docking anchor set (protein chains retained) plus 7 anchor-set proteins whose structures are predicted on first use — combines with the 20-target suite into the 39-target pool of the aptamer budget sweep. A five-protein mRNA deployment cohort (PTH, leptin, G-CSF, thrombopoietin and PLAT; single UniProt isoforms with accessions and lengths asserted at registration) and a six-protein aptamer deployment cohort (six anchor-set proteins) entered through the production user-form entry; their comparison conditions were completed under the identical budget-matched protocols, and the pooled 25- and 26-target analyses are reported with cohort splits disclosed. Two derived audit task sets support the claim-gating studies (Supplementary Methods): a 45-task aptamer set built on the nine-target anchor set — an earlier registered 20-task set carried byte-verbatim and extended by pre-registered construction rules to 24 trusted-deterministic tasks with exactly recomputable ground truth and 21 untrusted-predictor ranking and screening tasks spanning all nine targets — and a 20-task full-length mRNA set built from the same suite's real design assets (ten trusted-deterministic, ten untrusted-predictor).

External anchor sets ground the capability profiling and the biological-validity controls; every entry carries a role label and a per-entry source citation. Codon-usage controls: 20 wild-type CDSs fetched from NCBI RefSeq (per-target accessions recorded; translations verified against the benchmark proteins) together with 200 random synonymous controls (ten seeded uniform synonymous resamples per wild-type sequence). Vaccine-mRNA anchors: the assembled BNT162b2 and mRNA-1273 contigs of the published putative-sequence assemblies (Jeong and McCoy et al. 2021). Known aptamers: the thrombin-binding aptamer, pegaptanib and a SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid binder, each with its literature Kd. The aptamer binder/negative panels (95 entries over nine protein targets; 62 binders with measured Kd) and the 19 solved RNA–protein complexes (14 independent protein targets) are curated from literature affinities and RCSB Protein Data Bank structures. The IRES literature panel: nine experimentally validated IRES 5′UTRs — six viral (EMCV, HCV, poliovirus, FMDV, rhinovirus A1 and BVDV-1; RefSeq 5′UTR ranges) and three cellular (c-myc, VEGF-A and BiP; the validating publications include Stoneley et al. 1998, Stein et al. 1998 and Yang and Sarnow 1997) — against 50 random human 5′UTRs as negatives. Small-molecule binder/non-binder pairs: the theophylline aptamer (PDB 1O15; theophylline versus caffeine, roughly 10⁴-fold discrimination; Jenison et al. 1994), HIV-1 TAR (1ANR; aminoglycoside binders versus non-binder controls) and the guanine riboswitch (1Y27; guanine versus adenine and caffeine), with RNA sequences taken from the PDB records; the small-molecule predictor's held-out ROBIN and PDB test splits and its decoy case studies follow the predictor's published evaluation. An mRNA-stability panel of 400 constructs with measured reactivities and degradation rates derives from the stability predictor's upstream public test set. The measured-MRL 5′UTR anchor families derive from the published massively parallel translation assay (Sample et al., GEO accession GSE114002).

### Statistics

Paired comparisons used two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Cliff's δ as effect size; multiple-testing control used Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. AUROC confidence intervals used 10,000-fold bootstrap. The GenNav primary endpoints used exact paired McNemar tests on per-profile joint-criteria pass/fail. The baseline comparison (Fig. 6) uses the same exact paired McNemar tests on per-turn delivered validity (n = 96 turns) with Wilson 95% confidence intervals on claim false-positive rates; its per-workflow delivery counts, failure-mode decomposition and cost ledger are reported verbatim from the frozen comparison records. On mRNA, the pooled analysis covers n = 75 pairs = 25 targets × 3 profiles — the 20-target pre-registered cohort plus the five-target deployment cohort, whose navigation cells are the frozen form-session records and whose static conditions were completed under the identical budget-matched protocol (cohort split disclosed; the pre-registered 20-target cohort verdicts remain the inferential backbone). On aptamers, each arc segment uses n = 60 pairs over the 20-target suite, the decisive panel n = 36 pairs = 12 targets × 3 profiles and its confirmation n = 24, the expanded budget sweep a 39-target pool per cap, and the pooled summary n = 78 cells = 26 targets × 3 profiles, reported with cohort splits disclosed. Predictor ranking grades used Spearman correlation against measured values (Kd or MRL). Thresholds were pre-registered per campaign (for example, δ ≥ 0.3 for pilot GO gates; AUROC > 0.8; ρ ≥ 0.6; FDR q < 0.05 with |δ| ≥ 0.3 for design gains). Experiment orchestration was assisted by a coding agent external to the tested system.
